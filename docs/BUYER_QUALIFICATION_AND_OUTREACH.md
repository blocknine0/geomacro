# Buyer Qualification and Outreach Playbook

This is the founder-use playbook for turning Geomacro demos into qualified founding-pilot conversations.

## 1. First commercial wedge

Lead with a real decision, not the platform.

Best default wedge:

> Geopolitical and macro risk pre-flight for a cross-border payment, treasury action, country exposure, or automated financial workflow.

The buyer does not need to understand Arc, x402, prediction markets or agent terminology before the value is clear.

## 2. Priority account profile

### Tier 1: fast technical-pilot candidates

Target:

- fintech and payments companies;
- treasury software and cross-border infrastructure;
- financial-agent / automation platforms;
- API-first financial infrastructure;
- smaller or mid-market risk technology companies.

Look for a buyer who already owns a workflow where external risk context can influence approval, limits, routing, review or escalation.

### Tier 2: professional intelligence users

Target:

- corporate treasury;
- geopolitical risk and strategy teams;
- supply-chain and critical-mineral risk teams;
- commodities / macro research groups;
- insurers or financial institutions with a narrow innovation or design-partner path.

### Tier 3: strategic distribution / buyer universe

Target:

- financial-data and market-intelligence companies;
- enterprise-risk platforms;
- data / AI platforms;
- cybersecurity and risk vendors;
- banks, insurers and payment networks;
- cloud / agent infrastructure providers;
- consultancies and system integrators.

These accounts can be customers, partners, investors, licensing partners or strategic acquirers. Keep commercial and strategic paths open without implying that Geomacro is for sale in every conversation.

## 3. Qualification questions

A prospect becomes a real pilot candidate when at least four of these six questions have concrete answers:

1. What decision or workflow has the risk problem?
2. Which country, corridor, strategic resource or exposure matters?
3. Who uses or owns the output?
4. What happens today when geopolitical risk is unclear?
5. What would a useful output need to contain or trigger?
6. Who can approve a paid continuation if the pilot works?

If these remain vague, do a short discovery call or demo only. Do not commit engineering work.

## 4. First-touch message

Use a short problem-led note:

> I am building Geomacro, an explainable geopolitical and macro risk intelligence layer for operational and automated financial decisions. I am opening a small number of founding pilots around country/corridor risk and pre-flight decision context. If your team has a real treasury, payment, risk or agent workflow where geopolitical context matters, I would value 20 minutes to test whether the product fits it.

Do not lead with funding, crypto, prediction markets or a long feature list.

## 5. Technical-buyer variation

> Geomacro produces signed machine-readable country and corridor Risk Objects and applies them through a fail-closed Risk Gate before a customer-controlled action. I am looking for a small number of technical design partners with a real payment, treasury or agent workflow to test the integration. The current Arc/Circle x402 path is testnet technical proof; the commercial value is the external risk context and policy layer.

## 6. Risk / strategy-buyer variation

> Geomacro tracks geopolitical and macro developments as structured risk intelligence with evidence, confidence, freshness and change attribution. I am opening a small number of founding pilots around one real country, corridor or strategic exposure. The goal is not another news feed; it is to test whether the evidence trail and risk context materially improve an existing decision workflow.

## 7. After a positive reply

Do not immediately send a generic deck.

Reply with three questions:

1. What exact decision or workflow would you want to test?
2. Which country, corridor or exposure should we use?
3. Would the output be reviewed by a person, consumed by software, or both?

Then schedule a focused demo using `docs/EARLY_ACCESS_COMMERCIAL_PACKAGE.md`.

## 8. Demo-to-pilot close

At the end of the demo, ask:

> Is there one real workflow in your team where we could test this for 30 days with agreed success criteria?

If yes, define:

- champion;
- workflow;
- scope;
- output;
- technical path;
- success criteria;
- start date;
- pilot fee;
- continuation decision date.

Use `docs/FOUNDING_PILOT_PRICING_GUARDRAILS.md` internally for the commercial discussion.

## 9. Follow-up after the call

Send a one-page recap, not a broad company summary.

Include:

- the buyer problem as they described it;
- proposed country / corridor / exposure scope;
- what Geomacro will provide;
- what Geomacro will not provide;
- measurable pilot success criteria;
- proposed term and price;
- one next action and date.

## 10. Pipeline states

Use these states consistently:

- `IDENTIFIED`
- `CONTACTED`
- `REPLIED`
- `DISCOVERY`
- `DEMO_SCHEDULED`
- `DEMO_COMPLETE`
- `PILOT_PROPOSED`
- `PILOT_NEGOTIATION`
- `PILOT_ACTIVE`
- `PAID_CONTINUATION`
- `PARTNER_STRATEGIC`
- `ACQUISITION_SIGNAL`
- `NO_FIT`
- `DORMANT`

Every active opportunity should have one next action and one date. A contact without a next action is not an active deal.

## 11. Evidence discipline

Do not claim:

- customer adoption that does not exist;
- production SLA;
- third-party audit or certification;
- autonomous execution;
- full sanctions or logistics modelling;
- real-money production use from Arc Testnet evidence;
- commercial pricing based on the `0.001 USDC` x402 demonstration.

Use the measured launch evidence in `docs/SECURITY_RESILIENCE_LAUNCH_EVIDENCE.md` when needed.

## 12. Commercial objective

The first milestone is not maximum revenue. It is one credible paid pilot that produces evidence strong enough to answer:

1. who pays;
2. what exact decision they pay for;
3. what output they repeatedly need;
4. how much integration/support it takes;
5. whether they will pay again or expand.

Once that evidence exists, pricing, packaging, GTM and product prioritization can become more aggressive without relying on assumptions.
